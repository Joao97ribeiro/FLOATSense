Abstract

Fatigue limits the lifetime of an offshore wind turbine tower and is tracked from the bending moment along it, yet the strain gauges that measure it are rare and cover one section, while a tower-top accelerometer is standard. Reconstructing the moment at every height from that one sensor is a time-series problem, and no public benchmark scores it on the quantity engineers act on, the fatigue damage.

We introduce FLOATSense, a dataset and benchmark for fatigue load reconstruction on 22 MW floating offshore wind turbines: 718 k time series at 10 Hz from the 19,404 OpenFAST simulations of FLOATBench on three tower geometries (37 channels, 1,078 operating points × six turbulence seeds per tower), with the accelerometer and SCADA signals as inputs and the fore-aft moment at eleven heights as target. Two choices set it apart: a reconstruction is scored by the fatigue damage it implies rather than by waveform error, and the six realizations of an operating point are kept together, so a model that reads only the condition scores zero on the within-condition metric.

Twenty learned models and a field-validated physics baseline are evaluated within tower, across towers zero-shot and few-shot, and under sensor ablation. Sensor-level leaderboards mislead: the base does not separate the models and its leaders collapse at the top, where at the declared budget only three learned models beat a one-gain floor; longer training recovers the top for the two TCNs, not for the attention and state-space models; five simulations of a new tower recover its base, not its top; and a second accelerometer axis improves the top at every budget.

Limitations: the campaign is simulated, wind and waves are aligned, and only the fore-aft moment is scored.

Dataset

Each simulation holds 37 channels at 10 Hz over 1,000 s: tower-top accelerations, SCADA (rotor speed, blade pitch, power, hub wind speed), platform motions, wave elevation, and the fore-aft and side-side bending moments at 11 heights. Per tower: 1,078 operating points (22 wind speeds × 7 Hs × 7 Tp, IEC 61400-3-2 DLC 1.2) × 6 realizations = 6,468 simulations, plus 22 parked runs. Every file is keyed by the FLOATBench sim_id.

Task. From the gravity-corrected fore-aft tower-top acceleration, rotor speed, blade pitch, hub wind speed and a height z/H, reconstruct the fore-aft moment at that height over 400 to 1,000 s. One model serves the whole tower. True and reconstructed moments pass through the same pipeline: 3 Hz low-pass, rainflow counting, DNV-RP-C203 S-N curve and Miner's rule.

Resources

FLOATSense dataset

19,404 simulations on three towers in Parquet: series in 17 shards per tower (one row group per simulation), metadata, sections, reference damage and 66 parked runs. 24.0 GB, CC-BY-4.0. Released on publication.

FLOATSense code

Loader, damage scorer, physics baseline, 20 learned models, evaluation harness and the scripts behind every table and figure. MIT license.

Review subset

sim_id = 25 + 294k, k = 0…21, the same 22 simulations on each tower, all in the test split, with the complete tabular files, 14 checkpoints and the paper's per-simulation damage to compare against.

FLOATBench

The simulation campaign FLOATSense builds on: the same simulations, identifiers and regime-aware partition, with 582,120 tabular fatigue labels.

FLOAT

The fatigue-aware tower design framework behind the opt1 and opt2 geometries, integrated into WISDEM.

FLOAT-22-280-RWT-Semi (towers)

The fatigue-redesigned IEA 22 MW semi-submersible towers as ready-to-use OpenFAST models, the geometries simulated in FLOATSense.
