Abstract

Fatigue limits the lifetime of an offshore wind turbine tower and is tracked from the bending moment along it, yet the strain gauges that measure it are rare and cover one section, while a tower-top accelerometer is standard. Reconstructing the moment at every height from that one sensor is a time-series problem, and no public benchmark scores it on the quantity engineers act on, the fatigue damage. We introduce FLOATSense, a dataset and benchmark for fatigue load reconstruction on 22 MW floating offshore wind turbines: 718 k time series at 10 Hz from the 19,404 OpenFAST simulations of FLOATBench on three tower geometries (37 channels, 1,078 operating points × six turbulence seeds per tower), with the accelerometer and SCADA signals as inputs and the fore-aft moment at eleven heights as target.

FLOATSense is distinguished by two choices: a reconstruction is scored by the fatigue damage it implies rather than by waveform error, and the six realizations of an operating point are kept together, so a model that reads only the condition scores zero on the within-condition metric. Twenty learned models and a field-validated physics baseline are evaluated within tower, across towers zero-shot and few-shot, and under sensor ablation.

Sensor-level leaderboards mislead: the base does not separate the models and its leaders collapse at the top, where at the declared budget only three learned models beat a one-gain floor; longer training recovers the top for the two TCNs, not for the attention and state-space models; five simulations of a new tower recover its base, not its top; and a second accelerometer axis improves the top at every budget. Limitations: the campaign is simulated, wind and waves are aligned, and only the fore-aft moment is scored. Data, code and leaderboard are released.

Dataset

Operating envelope and simulation setup. The campaign of FLOATBench comprises 22 wind speeds and, at each, a 7 × 7 grid of 49 sea states in significant wave height Hs and peak period Tp, for 1,078 operating points. The IEA 22 MW turbine on a three-column semi-submersible is simulated with OpenFAST under IEC 61400-3-2 DLC 1.2, with aligned wind and waves. Each operating point has six realizations per tower, differing in the turbulence seed. A further 22 parked runs per tower, rotor parked and waves only, one at the median sea state of each wind speed, calibrate the physics baseline. Each run lasts 1,000 s at 10 Hz; evaluation uses the 400–1,000 s post-transient window (the scored window).

Tower geometries. The campaign is repeated on three towers of equal height sharing platform, controller and mooring, for 19,404 simulations: the IEA 22 MW baseline REF and the fatigue-aware redesigns OPT1 and OPT2 produced by FLOAT. REF is the flexible outlier (first fore-aft frequency 0.34 Hz against 0.57 and 0.54 Hz) and carries about 40 times the base lifetime damage of the two redesigns, so cross-tower shift comes at two levels.

Channels. Each simulation is an array of 10,001 samples by 37 channels: tower-top accelerations, raw and gravity-corrected (×4), SCADA signals (×4), platform motions (×6), wave elevation (×1), and fore-aft and side-side moments at 11 heights (×22).

Damage scoring. True and reconstructed moments are low-passed at 3 Hz and pass through the damage model that generated the FLOATBench labels: rainflow counting, the DNV-RP-C203 S–N curve and Miner's rule, with the radius and wall thickness of the section. The simulation campaign required about 19,400 CPU-hours across the three towers.

The 37 released time series of one simulation (1,000 s at 10 Hz): tower-top accelerations and SCADA as inputs, platform motions and wave elevation as context, and the fore-aft and side-side moments at 11 heights as targets.

Terms used on this page

Height z/HGauge height as a fraction of the tower height H = 149.4 m. The 11 gauges run from the base (0) to the top (1); one model serves all of them with z/H as an input.
Scored window400 to 1,000 s of each 1,000 s run, after the start-up transient; every metric is computed on it.
RealizationOne of the six turbulence seeds of an operating point: the same U, Hs, Tp with a different random wind field.
Fatigue damage DFraction of the fatigue life consumed (Miner's rule) over the 600 s window. Summed over 25 years with damage_weight, D = 1 means the section reaches its fatigue limit.
R² of log damageCoefficient of determination of log10 D̂ against log10 D over the test simulations: the primary ranking metric. It can be negative when the damage level is wrong.
×2 · ratio · MREFraction of simulations within a factor of two of the true damage; median ratio D̂/D (1 = unbiased); mean relative error.
Within-condition corr.Correlation between true and reconstructed damage across the six realizations of each operating point. A model that only recognizes the condition scores zero.
RegimesThe FLOATBench label of each test simulation on the wind and on the wave axis: In-train (IT), Interpolate (IP) or Extrapolate (EX).
Floor · physicsThe floor is one scalar gain from the tower-top acceleration to the moment. The physics baseline is the field method of Pimenta et al. (2024), a six-constant spectral gain calibrated on the parked runs and the training split, extended to every height.

Load the Data

One simulation is one row group of one shard, so it is read without scanning the file. The benchmark code wraps this in floatsense.release.

# one simulation with pyarrow
import pyarrow.parquet as pq
import pandas as pd

meta = pd.read_parquet("opt2/metadata.parquet")
shard = pq.ParquetFile("opt2/series-00000-of-00017.parquet")
sim = shard.read_row_group(0).to_pandas()   # sim_id 1, 10,001 rows
# with the benchmark loader
from floatsense.release import load_tower

tower = load_tower("data/FLOATSense", "opt2")
x = tower.load(2377)             # (10001, 37) float32
test_ids = tower.split_ids("test")
damage = tower.damage()          # sim_id x section_id

Benchmark Protocol

Task

Given the gravity-corrected fore-aft tower-top acceleration, three SCADA channels (rotor speed, blade pitch and hub wind speed) and a requested height z/H, reconstruct the fore-aft bending-moment history at that height over the scored window, 400 to 1,000 s. One model serves the full tower with z/H as a constant input channel: training samples one of the 11 target heights per example, testing evaluates all 11 against the OpenFAST reference histories. Only the fore-aft moment is scored; side-side moments are released but not scored.

Regime-aware partition

The regime-aware partition of FLOATBench, with the same simulations on the three towers: 1,728 train (18 wind speeds, inner 4 × 4 sea states) and 4,740 test, with all six seeds of an operating point on the same side. Each test simulation carries its FLOATBench regime label on the wind and on the wave axis. Unlike field measurements, the simulations give the true moment at every height and in extreme conditions, so extrapolation can be checked. The grid counts test simulations per cell (the same for every tower).

FLOATBench regime partition in the wind (left) and wave (right) planes.

Evaluation protocols

Within tower

Train and test on the same tower, on the regime-aware partition. Results along the 11 heights and per regime cell; the within-condition correlation tests whether a model tracks the realization or only the condition.

Cross tower

A model trained on one tower applied to each of the other two (six pairs). Zero-shot, and ten-shot: 100 adaptation steps on ten simulations of the target training split. The towers share the wind and wave realizations, so only the tower design changes.

Sensor ablation

Every simulation records all 37 channels, so other installations are compared on the same runs: the accelerometer alone, SCADA alone, both accelerometer axes, or the six platform motions added.

Models. Twenty learned models in eight families (convolutional, recurrent, attention, state-space, spectral, period-folding, pretrained encoders with a trained head and physics-anchored), among them a Prob-TCN with a heteroscedastic head, a condition-dependent spectral gain, and a hybrid that learns a bounded correction of the physics gain. The floor is one scalar gain from the tower-top acceleration to the moment. The physics baseline is the field method of Pimenta et al. (2024), extended to every height with a height factor.

Training. One recipe for every model, declared in advance: 50 epochs of Adam at 10−3, batch 16, mean squared error on the standardized moment, no weight decay, scheduler or early stopping, the last epoch scored. Seed 0 everywhere, three training seeds for the thirteen models within tower. Longer budgets (150 and 300 epochs) are probes on selected models.

Metrics. R2 of log10 damage (primary), median ratio, MRE, fraction within a factor of two, and within-condition correlation, with 95% bootstrap intervals that resample operating points (B = 1,000). A pairwise difference counts when the paired interval excludes zero on all three towers.

Results

Within tower · along the height

The base does not separate the models, the top does

At the base the sequence models are indistinguishable, and all beat the physics. Ten of the 22 entries reach R2 ≥ 0.95 at the base and the first seven lie within 0.023: Mamba, PatchTST and TCN score 0.99 to 1.00, the physics 0.967 / 0.944 / 0.961 and the floor 0.742 on average.

The top spreads the entries from −2.3 to 0.84, and the base ranking does not predict it. The rank correlation between base and top is −0.37 / 0.04 / 0.22. PatchTST and Mamba, first at the base, fall below zero at the top with no simulation within a factor of two; TCN keeps 0.775 / 0.774 / 0.732 and Prob-TCN 0.818 / 0.855 / 0.852. Only three learned models beat the floor at the top (0.70): Prob-TCN, TCN and FITS.

(a) R² along the tower; (b) top within a factor of two against wind speed; (c, d) cross tower; (e, f) sensor sets against the training budget.

A leaderboard at the base of the tower does not tell which model reconstructs its top.

Within tower · why the top fails

Training and variance, not missing information

TCN and Prob-TCN are under-trained at the top; the models that collapse stay collapsed. At 300 epochs TCN reaches 0.923 / 0.935 / 0.935 at the top and Prob-TCN 0.924 / 0.953 / 0.953, level with the physics on REF. S4D stays at 0.61 to 0.65 and PatchTST below zero. Trained for the top alone, TCN scores 0.90 / 0.94 / 0.93 and Prob-TCN 0.97 / 0.98 / 0.98, while PatchTST, S4D and Mamba still fail; a damage-aware loss rescues S4D (0.88 to 0.94).

The all-height TCN keeps 0.63 to 0.73 of the true variance at the top; restoring it with noise lifts the score, not the within-condition correlation.

Why the top fails, OPT2: variance ratio along the tower, spectra and coherence of the top moment.

At the declared budget a top score measures convergence as much as architecture; the collapse of the attention and state-space models survives a longer budget.

Within tower · by regime

At the top, low wind is the hard condition

Waves never cost. Wave extrapolation moves the fraction within a factor of two by −10 to +9 points for every model at any height. At the base, on extrapolated wind, the physics falls to 73 to 80% on the redesigns and Prob-TCN to 58 to 60%, while PatchTST and Mamba stay at 100%.

At the top the axis that matters is wind speed, not the regime label. TCN is within a factor of two on 0 to 12% of simulations below 10 m/s, in train or not, and on 92 to 99% above 13.5 m/s. Winds below 10 m/s carry only 3 to 5% of the lifetime top damage.

Median damage ratio per mean wind speed at the base (top row) and at the top (bottom row); gray band: factor-two range.

At the top, what decides the error is the wind speed, not the regime label.

Cross tower · zero-shot and few-shot

Zero-shot keeps the ranking, five simulations recover the base, not the top

Zero-shot fails on scale, not on ranking. The median zero-shot R2 at the base is 0.93 and 0.84 between the redesigns, 0.75 and 0.71 into REF, where the models over-predict (median ratio 2.6 and 2.5), and 0.41 and 0.48 out of it; the within-condition correlation stays at 0.84 to 0.95, so the realizations are ranked right and placed at the wrong level.

Five target simulations recover the base; no adaptation measured recovers the top. With five target simulations LSTM, Mamba, S4D, TCN and PatchTST score 0.89 to 1.00 at the base on every pair. At the top, after ten-shot adaptation, Prob-TCN averages 0.71, the floor 0.56 and TCN 0.44.

R² along the height, zero-shot (dotted) and after ten-shot adaptation (solid), one panel per ordered pair.

Five simulations of a new tower are enough at its base; no adaptation measured recovers its top.

Sensor ablation

The second accelerometer axis helps the top at every budget

At the top, TCN scores 0.94 with the platform motions added, 0.93 with both accelerometer axes, 0.76 with the task inputs, 0.68 with the accelerometer alone and 0.28 with SCADA alone; at 300 epochs 0.98, 0.97, 0.93, 0.84 and 0.74. The R2 gain of the second axis shrinks from 0.17 to 0.04, but at 300 epochs it still brings 87 to 90% of simulations within a factor of two against 72 to 76% with the task inputs, and within-condition correlation 0.94 to 0.95 against 0.82 to 0.88.

R² along the height under the five input sets, one panel per model, mean over the three towers.

The accelerometer is the sensor: with enough training one fore-aft axis gets the level of the top damage, and a second axis brings its fatigue metrics close to the base.

Lifetime damage

The top governs the redesigns, and it is where reconstruction fails

On the redesigns the top carries at least as much lifetime damage as the base (1.5 times on OPT1, 1.07 times on OPT2), so the height where every model struggles is the one that governs their fatigue life. At the base the sequence models reconstruct the lifetime damage within about 20%, and the physics is 10 to 44% conservative. At the top TCN reconstructs 0.61 to 0.66 of it and Prob-TCN 1.44 to 1.74, still 0.67 to 0.76 and 1.15 to 1.26 at 300 epochs.

Scope and Future Work

Simulation only. No measured load series enters the benchmark; the calibrated physics constants give a plausible platform pitch period, a plausibility check, not a validation. A leaderboard winner has still to survive sensor noise, sampling jitter and unmodeled dynamics.

One turbine family, aligned seas. All simulations are of one 22 MW turbine on one platform under DLC 1.2, so cross tower measures a change of tower design, not of turbine class or load case; the aligned waves leave side-side without direct wave excitation, which is why the side-side moment is released but not scored.

Mid-fidelity simulation. OpenFAST models the tower as a beam with engineering aerodynamics and hydrodynamics; higher-fidelity (FEA, CFD) re-runs of selected conditions will be released as new versions.

One recipe, short of convergence. All models share one budget of 50 epochs, equal and declared in advance, which is not converged at the top: every top number is a number at this budget, and the longer probes cover four models at 300 epochs.
